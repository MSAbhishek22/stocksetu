Your pickers are
walking too far.

In most godowns, goods are kept wherever they landed on the day they arrived. The tea and detergent that sell every day end up at the far end, and the slow stock sits next to the dock. Every order then becomes a long walk.

This page lets you watch that walk — and then fix it by moving the fast movers near the dock. That one change is called slotting.

A teaching model with simple rules. Every number on this page comes out of the model while you watch — nothing is typed in by hand.

The simple rules of this model

  • 72 products in 6 rack rows, 3 pickers, 1 dock. Orders ask for fast movers far more often than slow ones — roughly 10 : 4 : 1 for A : B : C.
  • Pickers walk at 4.5 m/s and take 2 seconds per pick. Speeds are set fast on purpose, so a shift fits into minutes.
  • Pick time counts from a picker taking an order to bringing it back to the dock. Time waiting in the dock queue is not counted as pick time.
  • Pickers travel along the cross-aisle and up the aisles — nobody walks through a rack.
  • No traffic jams, no empty bays, no breakage, no lunch breaks. Real warehouses are messier. But the direction of the result is the one real slotting projects see: keep the fast movers close, and the walking shrinks.
